# The NYC Food Guide — An NYU Student's Survival Manual

Welcome to the only syllabus that matters: eating well in New York City without going broke.
This guide is Manhattan-focused and walkable from NYU's Washington Square campus unless a
trip is noted. Prices are approximate as of 2026 and will make you nostalgic for 2019.

## Pizza: The Holy Grail

- Joe's Pizza, 7 Carmine St (West Village). The classic New York slice: thin, foldable,
  slightly charred. A few bucks a slice. Open late, which has saved many study nights.
  Celebrities love it; you will too.
- Bleecker Street Pizza, 69 7th Ave S (West Village). Famous for the Nonna Maria slice
  with fresh mozzarella. A strong challenger to Joe's.
- John's of Bleecker Street, 278 Bleecker St (West Village). Coal-fired brick-oven pies,
  whole pies only, no slices. Old-school dining room. Go with a group.
- Prince Street Pizza, 27 Prince St (Nolita). The spicy pepperoni square slice is the
  move. Worth the 15-minute walk from campus.

## Cheap Eats Near Campus (Under $12)

- Mamoun's Falafel, 119 St Marks Pl (East Village). Falafel sandwich around $6. An NYU
  institution since 1971. Open until about 4am on weekends.
- Xi'an Famous Foods, 81 St Marks Pl (East Village). Hand-pulled biang-biang noodles,
  spicy cumin lamb noodles around $11-13. Ask for extra chili if you're brave.
- 2 Bros Pizza (multiple locations). The "$1 slice" is now about $1.50 thanks to
  inflation, but it's still the cheapest hot food in Manhattan.
- Vanessa's Dumplings, 118 Eldridge St (Chinatown). Pork dumplings for a few dollars,
  plus the sesame pancake sandwich. Cash-friendly and fast.

## Bagels: A Food Group of Their Own

- Tompkins Square Bagels, 165 Avenue A (East Village). Creative cream cheese flavors,
  proper chewy bagels.
- Russ & Daughters, 179 E Houston St (Lower East Side). "Appetizing" (smoked fish and
  dairy, no meat). The classic lox on a bagel is a splurge but a rite of passage.
- Ess-a-Bagel, 831 3rd Ave (Midtown East). Enormous bagels, worth the trip uptown.

## Ramen and Slurpy Noodles

- Ippudo, 65 4th Ave (East Village). Rich tonkotsu (pork) ramen, around $18-20 a bowl.
  The East Village original is the classic NYC ramen experience.
- Totto Ramen, 366 W 52nd St (Hell's Kitchen). Chicken-based paitan broth, slightly
  lighter than tonkotsu. Expect a line at dinner.
- Los Tacos No.1, Chelsea Market, 75 9th Ave. Adobada (marinated pork) tacos around
  $4.50 each, carved off the trompo. Get three.

## The Deli Pilgrimage

- Katz's Delicatessen, 205 E Houston St (Lower East Side). Pastrami on rye, $25+.
  Go very hungry and split with a friend if you're cautious. Do not lose your meal
  ticket: you pay on the way out. This is where the famous scene from "When Harry Met
  Sally" was filmed.

## Dessert Emergencies

- Levain Bakery, 167 W 74th St (Upper West Side). The 6-ounce chocolate chip walnut
  cookie with a gooey center. Worth the subway ride uptown.
- Morgenstern's Finest Ice Cream, 2 Rivington St (Lower East Side). Burnt honey vanilla
  and rotating creative flavors.
- Cookie DO, 550 LaGuardia Pl (Greenwich Village, near campus). Edible cookie dough by
  the scoop. Dangerously close to Washington Square.

## Late Night (After Midnight)

- Mamoun's Falafel (St Marks): open until ~4am on weekends. The falafel never judges.
- Joe's Pizza (Carmine St): late slices for the walk home.
- Pommes Frites, 128 Macdougal St (Greenwich Village). Authentic Belgian fries with a
  wall of sauces. Open late.
- The late-night slice rule: if the slice has been sitting in the window, ask them to
  reheat it well-done.

## Birthday Dinner and Parents-Visiting Splurges

- L'Artusi, 228 W 10th St (West Village). Italian, famous olive oil cake. Book ahead;
  it's popular for a reason.
- Rubirosa, 235 Mulberry St (Nolita). Thin-crust Italian-American, known for the vodka
  pie with the tie-dye crust. Festive and delicious.
- Don Angie, 103 Greenwich Ave (West Village). The white whale: reservations are
  nearly impossible, but the Italian-American menu is legendary.

## Coffee and Study Cafes

- Birch Coffee (multiple locations, including East Village). Good espresso, laptop-friendly.
- Third Rail Coffee, 240 Sullivan St (Greenwich Village). Tiny, excellent, near campus.
- Rule of thumb: buy something every 2-3 hours if you're camping, and headphones in
  means do not disturb.

## Food Halls (Rainy-Day Strategy)

- Chelsea Market, 75 9th Ave. Los Tacos No.1 plus dozens of other vendors. Touristy
  but genuinely good.
- Essex Market, 88 Essex St (Lower East Side). Smaller and more local-feeling, great
  for groceries and prepared foods.

## Bodega 101

- A BEC is a bacon, egg, and cheese on a roll: the official New York breakfast
  sandwich, around $4-6. Salt-pepper-ketchup is the classic order.
- The chopped cheese is a Harlem and Bronx bodega legend: seasoned beef chopped on the
  grill with cheese, served on a hero roll. Worth a pilgrimage uptown.
- Bodega cats: respect them. They run the store and they know it.
- Most places take cards now, but cash still helps at the old-school counters.

## Student Survival Rules

1. Tip 18-20% at sit-down restaurants. At counters, tipping is optional but the jar
   exists and the staff notice.
2. Tap water is free everywhere: just ask.
3. If there's a line of locals, join it. If there's a line of tourists, keep walking.
4. The best food advice: the second visit is always better, because now you know what
   to order.
